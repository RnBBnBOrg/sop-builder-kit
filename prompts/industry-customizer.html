AI Prompt Pack: Industry Customizer

Use these prompts to adapt generic SOPs for your specific industry, compliance requirements, and business context.

Prompt 1: Adapt an SOP for Your Industry

Best when you have a general SOP template and need to tailor it for your specific field.

I have a general-purpose SOP that I need to customize for my specific industry.

Here is the SOP:
[Paste your SOP]

My industry: [e.g., "healthcare," "real estate," "e-commerce," "legal services," "construction," "SaaS"]
My specific niche: [e.g., "dental practice," "luxury vacation rentals," "B2B software," "family law"]
Company size: [e.g., "solo practitioner," "10-person team," "50+ employees"]
Regulatory environment: [e.g., "HIPAA compliance required," "SEC regulated," "no specific regulations," "state licensing requirements"]

Customize this SOP for my industry by:
1. Adding industry-specific steps or checkpoints I'm missing
2. Adjusting terminology to match my industry's standards
3. Adding any compliance or regulatory requirements relevant to this process
4. Including industry-specific quality standards
5. Noting common industry pitfalls for this type of process
6. Adjusting timelines and expectations to industry norms

Keep the core structure but make it feel like it was written by someone who understands [my industry].

Prompt 2: Add Compliance Requirements to an SOP

Best when you need to make sure your SOP meets specific regulatory or compliance standards.

I need to add compliance and regulatory requirements to this SOP.

Here is the SOP:
[Paste your SOP]

Compliance frameworks that apply:
- [e.g., "HIPAA," "GDPR," "SOC 2," "PCI-DSS," "OSHA," "state bar requirements," "FDA regulations"]

Specific requirements I know about:
[List any specific requirements you're already aware of, or say "I'm not sure — help me identify them"]

For each compliance framework:
1. Identify which steps in the SOP have compliance implications
2. Add specific compliance checkpoints where required
3. Note documentation requirements (what records to keep, for how long)
4. Add required approvals or sign-offs
5. Include any mandatory waiting periods or cool-down periods
6. Add data handling or privacy requirements if applicable

Also:
- Flag any steps that could create compliance risk if done incorrectly
- Add a compliance checklist I can use for audits
- Note anything this SOP is missing that a regulator would expect to see

Important: I'll verify all regulatory requirements with my [lawyer / compliance officer / industry advisor]. Mark anything you're uncertain about with [VERIFY WITH COMPLIANCE ADVISOR].

Prompt 3: Scale an SOP for a Growing Team

Best when your process worked for a small team but needs to scale.

This SOP was written when our team was small. We're growing and it needs to scale.

Here is the current SOP:
[Paste your SOP]

Current situation:
- Team size when SOP was written: [e.g., "2 people"]
- Current team size: [e.g., "8 people"]
- Expected team size in 12 months: [e.g., "15-20 people"]
- Current pain points: [e.g., "bottlenecks at approval step," "inconsistent quality," "too many people involved in every decision"]

Redesign this SOP for scale by:
1. Identifying bottlenecks that will get worse as the team grows
2. Adding delegation rules — what can be handled at what level
3. Creating role-based variants (what does this process look like for a junior vs. senior team member?)
4. Adding batch processing options for high-volume scenarios
5. Building in quality controls that don't require a single person to review everything
6. Adding escalation paths for exceptions
7. Suggesting tools or automation that become worthwhile at this scale

Give me:
- The updated SOP for current size
- Notes on what to change at [next milestone] team size
- A "we've outgrown this SOP" checklist — signs that it needs revision again

Prompt 4: Create Industry-Specific Quality Standards

Best when you need to define what "good" looks like for your specific industry.

I need industry-specific quality standards for my business processes.

Industry: [your industry]
Process area: [e.g., "client deliverables," "service delivery," "product quality," "customer communication"]

What we deliver: [brief description of your main outputs]
Who judges quality: [e.g., "clients," "regulatory bodies," "internal stakeholders," "end users"]

Create quality standards that include:
1. Industry benchmarks — what's standard, what's above average, what's excellent
2. Specific, measurable criteria for each quality level (not vague "high quality" — actual metrics)
3. Common quality failures in this industry and how to prevent them
4. A grading rubric I can use to evaluate work before it goes out
5. Client-facing quality promises I can make with confidence
6. Internal quality targets that are ambitious but achievable

Format this as:
- A quality standards document (the reference)
- A quick-check rubric (the daily tool)
- A quarterly quality review template (the measurement)

Prompt 5: Adapt SOPs for Remote vs. In-Person Teams

I need to adapt this SOP for a [remote / hybrid / distributed] team.

Here is the SOP (currently written for [in-person / co-located] teams):
[Paste your SOP]

Team setup:
- Team locations: [e.g., "all remote across US time zones," "hybrid — 3 days in office, 2 remote," "distributed across US, Europe, and Asia"]
- Communication tools: [e.g., "Slack, Zoom, Notion, Google Drive"]
- Working hours overlap: [e.g., "9am-1pm EST," "minimal overlap — mostly async"]

Adapt this SOP by:
1. Replacing in-person steps with remote equivalents (don't just add "on Zoom")
2. Adding async-friendly alternatives for time-sensitive steps
3. Building in explicit communication checkpoints (things that happen naturally in-person but get lost remotely)
4. Adding documentation requirements that replace informal in-person knowledge sharing
5. Adjusting timelines for async communication delays
6. Adding timezone-aware scheduling guidance

Also note:
- Which steps work better synchronously vs. asynchronously
- Where video is actually needed vs. where a Slack message is better
- How to handle handoffs across time zones

Prompt 6: Create a Client-Facing Version of an Internal SOP

Best when you want to show clients your process without revealing internal details.

I have an internal SOP and I want to create a client-facing version that shows our professionalism without revealing our internal workings.

Here is the internal SOP:
[Paste your SOP]

Client context:
- What clients care about: [e.g., "timeline, quality, communication, deliverables"]
- What clients don't need to see: [e.g., "internal review steps, pricing calculations, tool details"]
- Format needed: [e.g., "one-pager for proposals," "onboarding document," "process overview for website"]

Create a client-facing version that:
1. Shows the key stages of the process from the client's perspective
2. Highlights what the client can expect at each stage (deliverables, timelines, communication)
3. Explains what the client needs to provide and when
4. Removes all internal details (tools, team names, approval chains)
5. Projects professionalism and builds confidence
6. Includes clear contact points and escalation options

Give me both:
- A visual process overview (describe it — I'll design it) with stage names and key milestones
- A written process description suitable for proposals or onboarding docs

Prompt 7: Localize an SOP for Different Markets

I need to adapt this SOP for a different market or region.

Here is the SOP:
[Paste your SOP]

Original market: [e.g., "United States"]
Target market: [e.g., "United Kingdom," "Canada," "Australia," "European Union"]

Adapt for the target market by:
1. Adjusting for local regulations and legal requirements
2. Updating terminology and spelling conventions
3. Adjusting for local business customs and communication norms
4. Updating any currency, measurement, or date format references
5. Noting timezone and business hours differences
6. Flagging any steps that may not work in the target market (and suggesting alternatives)

Also identify:
- Any certifications or registrations needed in the target market
- Cultural differences that could affect how this process is received
- Local tools or platforms that are preferred over the ones currently mentioned

Prompt 8: Build an SOP Library Plan for Your Business

Best when you're starting from zero and want to know which SOPs to create first.

I want to build a library of SOPs for my business but I don't know where to start.

Business type: [your industry and business model]
Team size: [current team size]
Current documentation: [e.g., "nothing," "some scattered docs," "a few outdated SOPs"]
Biggest operational pain points: [e.g., "inconsistent client experience," "things falling through cracks," "too dependent on one person"]

Help me:
1. List every SOP my business should eventually have (be comprehensive)
2. Categorize them by department/function
3. Prioritize them into three tiers:
   - Tier 1: Create this week (highest impact, prevents fires)
   - Tier 2: Create this month (important for consistency)
   - Tier 3: Create this quarter (nice to have, builds long-term operational excellence)
4. For each Tier 1 SOP, give me a one-paragraph brief I can use to draft it
5. Suggest a realistic timeline for building the full library
6. Recommend a system for maintaining and updating SOPs over time

Consider: client-facing processes, internal operations, financial processes, HR/people processes, technology and tools, and emergency/contingency plans.
